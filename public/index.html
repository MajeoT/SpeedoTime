Why this exists: psychologists call it the time-saving bias. People overestimate the time saved by speeding up from an already high speed, and underestimate the time saved by speeding up from a low one. Going 30 → 40 mph saves far more than 70 → 80 mph over the same distance, because trip time is distance ÷ speed, a curve that flattens out. Put the two needles 10 mph apart and slide them up the dial together to watch the saving shrink.
